# Asset allocation

## Why the mix matters more than the picks
Asset allocation is how you split money between asset classes: **equity** (shares and equity funds), **debt** (bonds, debt funds, FDs) and **cash-like** holdings (liquid funds, savings). Studies of diversified portfolios consistently find that the mix explains most of the ups and downs in your returns, far more than which individual funds you pick.

- **Equity** grows fastest over long periods but can fall 30-50% in a bad year.
- **Debt and FDs** grow slowly but steadily and cushion falls.
- **Liquid** holdings keep money ready for near-term needs.

## What decides your mix
1. **Time horizon**: money needed within 3 years should not be in equity, because markets can stay down for 2-3 years. The longer the horizon, the more equity you can hold.
2. **Risk capacity**: your age, income stability, dependents and emergency cushion decide how much loss you can *afford*.
3. **Risk tolerance**: how much loss you can *stomach* without panic-selling. Selling after a crash locks in the loss.

A classic rule of thumb is "100 minus your age" in equity: a 30-year-old would hold about 70%.

## Diversify within each asset class
Within equity, spread across company sizes (large, mid, small) and avoid holding several funds that own the same stocks. Within debt, prefer high-quality, short-to-medium duration funds and FDs from sound banks.

## Rebalancing
Markets move your mix away from the target. Once a year (or when an asset drifts more than 5 percentage points), sell a little of what has grown and add to what has lagged. This forces you to "buy low, sell high" and keeps risk in check. Using new SIP money to rebalance avoids selling and the tax that comes with it.

## How Wealth Wise decides your mix
Equity starts at 10% plus 8% for every point of risk score above 1, then is capped by age (110 minus age) and by horizon: 0% under 3 years, 30% under 5, 55% under 7. Liquid funds get 5% (10% for short horizons). The rest is split between FDs and debt funds, with more FDs for lower risk scores.
