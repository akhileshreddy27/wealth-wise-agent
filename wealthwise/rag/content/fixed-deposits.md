# Fixed deposits

## How an FD works
A fixed deposit locks a lump sum with a bank for a chosen tenure (from 7 days to 10 years) at a rate fixed on the day you open it. Most banks compound interest **quarterly** for cumulative FDs, so a 7% FD earns slightly more than 7% a year effectively. Rates differ by tenure; banks often pay the most for 1-3 year deposits.

A **recurring deposit (RD)** is the monthly version: a fixed amount every month at a fixed rate, useful when you save from salary rather than having a lump sum.

## Safety: DICGC insurance
Bank deposits are insured by **DICGC** (a subsidiary of the RBI) up to **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank**, covering principal and interest together. Small finance banks often pay 0.5-1% more than large banks and are covered by the same insurance, but their higher rates reflect higher risk, so keep amounts above ₹5 lakh per bank in larger banks or split them across banks.

Post Office time deposits are backed by the Government of India.

## Tax on FD interest
- FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your **slab rate**, every year, even on cumulative FDs where you receive nothing until maturity.
- Banks deduct **TDS at 10%** once your interest from that bank crosses **₹50,000 in a financial year (₹1 lakh for senior citizens)**. TDS is only an advance tax: you still report the interest and pay any extra tax due. If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can submit Form 15G (or 15H for seniors) to avoid TDS.
- **Senior citizens** (60+) usually get about 0.25-0.5% extra interest.

## Breaking an FD early
Premature withdrawal is usually allowed with a penalty, typically 0.5-1% off the rate applicable for the period the money actually stayed. Tax-saver FDs cannot be broken before 5 years.

## Tax-saver FD
A 5-year tax-saver FD qualifies for the Section 80C deduction (up to ₹1.5 lakh a year, only under the old tax regime). It has a 5-year lock-in and its interest is still taxable.
