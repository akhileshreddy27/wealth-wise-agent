# Budgeting with the 50/30/20 rule

## What the rule says
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to split your take-home income each month:
- **50% for needs**: rent, groceries, electricity and phone bills, transport to work, insurance premiums and loan EMIs. These are costs you must pay to live and keep earning.
- **30% for wants**: eating out, shopping, streaming subscriptions, travel and entertainment. Life is better with them, but you can cut back.
- **20% for savings and investing**: SIPs, emergency fund top-ups, extra loan prepayments and long-term goals.

It is a guideline, not a law. In expensive cities rent alone can take 35-40% of income, so many people start at 60/20/20 and work towards 50/30/20 as their income grows.

## Why investing counts as saving
A SIP debit leaves your bank account like any other payment, but it is not spending: the money is still yours, just invested. Wealth Wise therefore counts SIPs and investments in the savings bucket, not in needs or wants. Your real savings rate is income minus spending, where spending excludes investments.

## How to make it work
1. **Pay yourself first.** Schedule your SIP for the day after salary credit, so saving happens before spending.
2. **Track a few months of real data.** Most people underestimate wants by 20-30%. Import a bank statement to see actual numbers.
3. **Attack the biggest want, not every want.** Cutting one large category (often food delivery or shopping) is easier than trimming ten small ones.
4. **Watch recurring payments.** Unused subscriptions quietly add up; a ₹649 monthly plan is about ₹7,800 a year.

## How Wealth Wise uses this
The Expenses page sorts every category into needs, wants or savings, compares your split with 50/30/20, flags budgets you've crossed, spots spending spikes and recurring subscriptions, and shows what a 20% cut in your biggest discretionary category could grow to if invested.
