# Index funds

## What an index fund does
An index fund simply copies a market index. A Nifty 50 index fund holds the 50 companies in the Nifty 50 in the same proportions as the index, so its return is the index return minus a small cost. No fund manager tries to pick winners; this is called **passive investing**.

## Why they are popular
- **Low cost**: direct-plan expense ratios are often around 0.1-0.3% a year, versus 0.5-1% or more for active funds.
- **No manager risk**: you won't underperform the market because of a fund manager's bad calls.
- **Transparent**: you always know what you own.
- **Hard to beat**: S&P Dow Jones Indices' SPIVA India scorecards have repeatedly shown that a majority of active large-cap funds underperform their benchmark over 5-10 year periods, largely because of costs.

## What to compare between index funds
All Nifty 50 index funds hold the same stocks, so compare:
- **Expense ratio**: lower is better.
- **Tracking error**: how closely the fund follows the index; lower is better.
- **Fund size**: larger funds usually track more smoothly.

## Which index?
- **Nifty 50 / Sensex**: India's largest companies. A good core holding.
- **Nifty Next 50**: the next 50 large companies; more volatile.
- **Nifty Midcap 150 / Smallcap 250**: higher growth potential and much higher volatility.
- **Nifty 500**: a broad market fund covering large, mid and small companies.

## How Wealth Wise uses this
The "core" equity sleeve is filled with index and large-cap funds. Index funds are placed by what they track: a Nifty 50 fund is core, a Midcap 150 index fund goes into the mid-cap sleeve, so a conservative profile never gets mid-cap risk through the back door.
