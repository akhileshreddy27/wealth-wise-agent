# Saving tax: 80C, ELSS and the two tax regimes

## Old vs new tax regime
Individuals choose between two regimes each year:
- The **new regime** (the default) has lower slab rates and a higher standard deduction for salaried people, but allows almost no deductions. Under the slabs announced in Budget 2025, a rebate makes taxable income up to **₹12 lakh** effectively tax-free.
- The **old regime** has higher rates but allows deductions such as **Section 80C** (up to ₹1.5 lakh a year), 80D (health insurance) and home-loan interest.

As a rough guide, the old regime only wins if your deductions are large, for example a full 80C plus a home loan and health insurance. Compare both each year with the Income Tax Department's calculator. From 1 April 2026 the new Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act and renumbers sections; the widely used names like "80C" refer to the old numbering.

## What counts under 80C
- **EPF** contributions deducted from salary (often fill much of the limit already)
- **PPF**: 15-year government scheme with tax-free interest
- **ELSS** mutual funds: 3-year lock-in, the shortest among 80C options
- **Tax-saver FD**: 5-year lock-in, taxable interest
- Life insurance premiums, children's tuition fees, home-loan principal, NSC, Sukanya Samriddhi

## ELSS: equity-linked savings schemes
ELSS funds are diversified equity funds with a 3-year lock-in for each SIP instalment (each monthly investment is locked for 3 years from its own date). They offer equity-like growth and volatility. Gains on redemption are taxed like other equity funds.

## How Wealth Wise uses this
Wealth Wise's plan doesn't depend on your tax regime. If you file under the old regime and have 80C room left, you can direct part of your index or flexi-cap SIP (up to ₹12,500 a month, ₹1.5 lakh a year) into an ELSS fund instead. Check how much of your 80C limit EPF and insurance already use before adding more.
