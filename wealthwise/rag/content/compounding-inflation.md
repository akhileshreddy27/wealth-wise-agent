# Compounding and inflation

## Compounding: returns on your returns
Compounding means your returns start earning returns. If ₹1,00,000 grows 12% in a year it becomes ₹1,12,000; the next year's 12% is earned on ₹1,12,000, not on the original amount.

Over long periods this snowballs. At 12% a year, money roughly doubles every 6 years (the **Rule of 72**: 72 ÷ 12 = 6). Starting early matters more than starting big: someone who invests for 30 years ends up with far more than someone who invests twice as much for 15 years.

## Inflation: the silent tax
Inflation is the rise in prices over time. India's retail (CPI) inflation has typically averaged around 5-6% a year over the long run, and the RBI targets 4% within a 2-6% band.

At 6% inflation, something that costs ₹1,00,000 today costs about ₹1,79,000 in 10 years. Money kept in a savings account earning less than inflation is **losing purchasing power** every year, even though the balance grows.

## Real returns
What matters is the **real return**: your return minus inflation (and after tax). An FD paying 7% when inflation is 6% gives a real return of only about 1% before tax, and less after tax at your slab rate. Equity has historically delivered higher real returns over long periods, which is why long-term goals usually need some equity.
