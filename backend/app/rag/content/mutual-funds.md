# Mutual funds 101

## What a mutual fund is
A mutual fund pools money from many investors and a professional fund manager invests it in stocks, bonds or both, according to the fund's stated objective. Each investor owns **units**; the price of one unit is the **NAV** (net asset value), calculated at the end of every business day as the fund's total assets minus expenses, divided by units outstanding.

Funds in India are regulated by **SEBI**, and the industry body **AMFI** publishes NAVs daily. Every fund must show a **riskometer**, from Low to Very High risk.

## Direct vs regular plans
Every fund has two versions:
- **Regular plan**: bought through a distributor, who earns a commission paid out of the fund's expenses.
- **Direct plan**: bought directly from the AMC or through a direct platform, with no commission, so the **expense ratio is lower**, often by 0.5-1% a year.

The portfolio is identical; only the cost differs. Over 20 years, a 1% annual cost difference can reduce the final corpus by around 15-20%. Wealth Wise only tracks Direct-Growth plans.

## Growth vs IDCW
- **Growth**: profits stay invested and the NAV rises. Best for long-term compounding.
- **IDCW** (income distribution cum capital withdrawal, formerly "dividend"): the fund pays out part of your own money from time to time, and payouts are taxed at your slab rate.

## Main categories
SEBI defines fund categories so funds are comparable:
- **Equity**: large cap (top 100 companies), mid cap (101-250), small cap (251 onwards), flexi cap (any size), ELSS (tax-saving, 3-year lock-in), index funds (track an index).
- **Debt**: liquid and overnight (days to weeks), short duration, corporate bond, gilt (government bonds).
- **Hybrid**: mix equity and debt, such as balanced advantage funds that shift the mix with market valuations.

## Costs and exit loads
The **expense ratio** is charged daily inside the NAV. Many equity funds also charge an **exit load** (often 1%) if you redeem within a year. Check both on the fund's factsheet.
