# Judging a fund: returns, risk and consistency

## Returns
- **CAGR (compound annual growth rate)**: the steady yearly return that would turn the starting value into the ending value. A fund that went from ₹100 to ₹200 in 5 years has a CAGR of about 14.9%.
- **Trailing returns** (1, 3, 5 years) depend heavily on the start and end dates. One great or terrible year can distort them.
- **Rolling returns** fix this: look at every 1-year window over, say, 3 years. "Positive in 95% of 1-year periods" says more about consistency than a single 3-year number.

## Risk
- **Volatility (standard deviation)**: how widely returns swing around the average, annualised. Higher means a bumpier ride.
- **Maximum drawdown**: the largest fall from a peak to a later low, for example -25%. It shows the worst loss an investor who bought at the top would have sat through.

## Risk-adjusted return
- **Sharpe ratio**: (return − risk-free rate) ÷ volatility. It measures the extra return earned per unit of risk taken. Above 1 is very good for equity funds over 3 years; compare only within the same category.
- **Sortino ratio**: like Sharpe, but it only counts downside volatility, since investors don't mind upside surprises.

Wealth Wise uses a risk-free rate of about 6.5%, close to the Indian 91-day Treasury bill yield.

## How Wealth Wise ranks funds
Funds are compared only with peers: the same SEBI category, and index funds only with index funds tracking the same segment. The score weights 3-year Sharpe 25%, and Sortino, 3-year CAGR, 5-year CAGR, rolling 1-year consistency and maximum drawdown 15% each, as percentiles within the peer group. Missing data (a fund younger than 5 years) counts as neutral and is flagged, and scores in very small peer groups are pulled toward 50 because ranking two or three funds is weak evidence.

Past performance doesn't guarantee future returns. Also check the expense ratio and fund manager changes on the AMC factsheet.
