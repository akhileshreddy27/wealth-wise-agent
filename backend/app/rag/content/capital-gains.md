# Tax on investment gains

## Equity shares and equity mutual funds
Equity-oriented funds (at least 65% in Indian equity) and listed shares are taxed when you sell:
- **Short-term capital gains (STCG)**, held 12 months or less: **20%** on the gain.
- **Long-term capital gains (LTCG)**, held more than 12 months: **12.5%** on gains above **₹1.25 lakh a year**. The first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term equity gains each financial year is tax-free, combined across all your equity funds and shares.

These rates apply to sales from 23 July 2024 onwards (Budget 2024). Each SIP instalment has its own purchase date, so holding periods are counted per instalment, oldest units sold first.

**Tax harvesting**: selling and re-buying equity funds to book up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each year can reduce future tax. Mind exit loads and the ELSS lock-in.

## Debt mutual funds
For debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023, gains are added to your income and taxed at your **slab rate**, whatever the holding period. This makes debt funds similar to FDs for tax, though you pay tax only when you sell, while FD interest is taxed every year.

## Fixed deposits
FD interest is taxed at your slab rate each year as it accrues. See the fixed deposits lesson for TDS rules.

## Hybrid funds
Tax depends on the equity share: funds with 65% or more in equity (most balanced advantage funds) are taxed like equity funds.

## Other points
- **IDCW (dividend) payouts** from funds are taxed at your slab rate.
- Securities transaction tax (STT) and stamp duty are small costs built into the transaction.
- Tax rules change with each Budget; confirm current rates on the Income Tax Department website before acting.
