# Goal-based investing

## Start from the goal, not the product
Goal-based investing gives every rupee a job: a house down payment in 5 years, a child's education in 12, retirement in 25. Each goal has an amount, a date and a priority, and that decides how to invest for it.

## Four steps
1. **Price the goal in today's money**, for example ₹25 lakh for a down payment.
2. **Inflate it** to the goal date. At 6% inflation, ₹25 lakh in 10 years is about ₹44.8 lakh.
3. **Pick the mix for the horizon.** Short goals (under 3 years) belong in debt and FDs; long goals can hold mostly equity, which changes the expected return.
4. **Work out the monthly SIP** needed at that expected return, after counting money already saved for the goal.

## Make it achievable
If the SIP is more than you can invest, you have four levers: invest longer, start with what you can and **step up** the SIP each year with your salary, lower the target, or reach for a slightly riskier mix if your horizon genuinely allows it. Never take more risk on a short-horizon goal to close a gap.

## Glide path
As a long goal gets closer, move money gradually from equity to debt (for example over the last 3 years) so a market crash right before the goal date can't derail it.

## How Wealth Wise uses this
Ask the assistant something like "I need ₹20 lakh in 8 years for a house down payment". It adjusts the target for 6% inflation, then uses the SIP formula to find the monthly amount, with a return assumption that suits the horizon: debt-like returns for goals under 3 years, a balanced mix for 3-7 years, and equity-like returns beyond that. Compare the answer with the monthly surplus on your Plan page.
