# SIP: systematic investment plans

## What a SIP is
A SIP (systematic investment plan) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly, through an automatic bank mandate (e-NACH or UPI AutoPay). You can start with as little as ₹100-500 a month in most funds, and you can pause, change or stop it any time; a SIP is a payment instruction, not a contract.

## Rupee cost averaging
Because the amount is fixed, a SIP buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. Over time this averages out your purchase cost and removes the need to time the market. It does not guarantee profit, but it reduces the risk of investing a large amount just before a fall.

## Why SIPs build discipline
- **Automatic**: money is invested before you can spend it.
- **Behavioural**: you keep buying during market falls, which is when long-term returns are made, instead of panicking.
- **Scalable**: a **step-up SIP** raises the amount every year (say 10%) as your salary grows, which makes a large difference to the final corpus.

## How SIP maths works
A monthly SIP of P for n months at a monthly rate r grows to roughly P × [((1 + r)^n − 1) / r] × (1 + r). For example, ₹10,000 a month for 10 years at 12% a year grows to about ₹23.2 lakh, on ₹12 lakh invested.

Returns from equity funds are not fixed: 12% is a long-run assumption, and individual years can be negative.

## How Wealth Wise uses this
Your monthly investable surplus is split into SIPs across sleeves (index, flexi-cap, debt and so on) based on your asset allocation, and sleeves under ₹500 a month are merged because that is the usual SIP minimum. The assistant can also work the formula backwards to find the SIP a goal needs.
