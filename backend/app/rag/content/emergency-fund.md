# Emergency fund: your financial shock absorber

## What it is
An emergency fund is money set aside only for genuine surprises: a job loss, a medical bill, an urgent family need or a major home or vehicle repair. It is not for holidays or planned purchases.

Without one, an emergency forces bad choices: breaking long-term investments at a loss, borrowing on a credit card at 36-42% a year, or taking a personal loan.

## How much you need
The common guideline is **6 months of essential expenses**. Use more (9-12 months) if:
- your income is irregular (freelance, business, commission-based),
- you are the only earner or have dependents,
- your job is in a volatile industry.

Count expenses, not income: rent, groceries, bills, EMIs, insurance and school fees.

## Where to keep it
The money must be **safe and quick to withdraw**, so returns come second:
- **Savings account or sweep-in FD**: instant access; the sweep-in moves extra balance into an FD automatically.
- **Liquid mutual funds**: invest in very short-term debt; redemptions usually reach your bank the next working day, and some allow small instant withdrawals.
- **Short FDs**: fine for part of it, but breaking an FD early usually costs a 0.5-1% penalty on the rate.

Avoid equity funds or stocks for this money: markets can be down exactly when you need cash.

## Why Wealth Wise builds it first
The recommendation engine checks whether your savings cover 6 months of expenses. If not, it sends your surplus to a liquid fund first: all of it if the gap can be closed within about 3 months, otherwise half, so that long-term investing still starts. Once the fund is full, the full surplus goes into your investment plan.
