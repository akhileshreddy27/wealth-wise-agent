# Reading a stock: valuation, quality and momentum

## Valuation: is it cheap or expensive?
- **P/E (price-to-earnings)**: share price ÷ earnings per share. A P/E of 25 means you pay ₹25 for every ₹1 of annual profit. Compare within the same industry; fast-growing companies usually trade at higher P/Es. A negative P/E means the company is making losses.
- **P/B (price-to-book)**: price ÷ book value (net assets) per share. Most useful for banks and asset-heavy businesses.
- **Dividend yield**: annual dividend ÷ price.

A low P/E is not automatically a bargain: the market may expect profits to fall ("value trap").

## Quality: is it a good business?
- **ROE (return on equity)**: profit ÷ shareholders' equity. Consistently above 15% suggests a strong business.
- **Debt-to-equity**: borrowings ÷ equity. Below 0.5 is comfortable for most non-financial companies; banks are different because lending borrowed money is their business.
- **Net profit margin**: profit ÷ revenue. Higher margins suggest pricing power.

## Momentum and risk
- **Momentum**: stocks that have risen over the past 6-12 months have historically tended to keep outperforming for a while. It is a well-documented factor, but trends can reverse sharply.
- **Volatility**: how much the price swings, as an annualised standard deviation of daily returns. 20% is typical for a large Indian company.
- **Beta**: how much the stock moves with the market. Beta 1.2 means it tends to move 12% when the Nifty moves 10%; below 1 means calmer than the market.

## How Wealth Wise scores stocks
Each NIFTY 50 stock gets a percentile (0-100) within NIFTY 50 for four factors: **value** (low P/E, low P/B), **quality** (high ROE, low debt, high margin), **momentum** (6-month and 1-year return) and **low volatility** (low volatility, low beta). Conservative profiles weight quality and low volatility more; aggressive profiles weight momentum more. Conservative investors only see stocks with volatility up to 25% and beta up to 1.0, and picks are capped at two per sector. Scores rank stocks against each other; they don't predict prices.

## Direct stocks vs funds
Picking stocks takes time and concentration risk is high. Most investors are better served by keeping direct stocks to a small slice (the app suggests 10-20% of equity, and only if you already invest) and the rest in diversified funds.
